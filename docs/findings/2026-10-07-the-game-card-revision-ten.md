## 2. What the clause says

**The card now carries which of its two headline contrasts resolves, and the answer is the method's.** On every one of
the ten rolls the buffer's mean diagonal over its anchor is positive at **8.27** sigma or more -- the weakest instance
four times the bar, and far clear of the **3.30** that is `e276`'s weakest on the state read-out -- and the ten span
**0.0708** across an order's change, two redraws and two widths. Read with the `ledger` and `width` clauses, a reader of
the card has the whole of what this line has measured on its world: the buffer's advantage is the sequence's at the first
position and the storage's everywhere else, the anchors have no standing and a price, the width peaks at eight, and the
**method** contrast is the one that holds at every manipulation. **The basis contrast, which is the paper's question, is
not in any of the three clauses and has never resolved** -- and this revision is the first place in the card where a
clause states a contrast that does.

**And the forgetting half of the clause is as uniform as the accuracy half.** All ten are negative, **-0.1776** to
**-0.2615**, so on this world the buffer is not merely the more accurate arm in each roll: the two currencies do not trade
off anywhere in the ten, and the clause carries both so that a reader who wants only one cannot get it without the other.

**And the clause's two manipulations that move it most are the widths, in the direction the `width` clause carries.** The
smallest two of the ten are **+0.1250** at sixteen columns and **+0.1483** at four against **+0.1896** at the card's own
eight, and revision 9's clause already records that the arms' own effects peak at eight -- so the two clauses together
say that the width moves the arms' **advantage over each other** less than it moves either arm, and the **0.0708** left
is what CA4's **0.10** was registered to be able to contain. The four other manipulations move the contrast by
**0.0045** to **0.0301**.

**And the clause's count is a count of rolls and not of readings.** Three of the ten are matched-random rolls and the two
anchors have been found **0.0042**, **0.0062**, **0.0094**, **0.0101** and **0.0108** apart by the units that read them
directly, so a reader who multiplies **10** by **20** replicates gets 200 numbers from at most **7** independent cells.
The clause says *ten rolls* and the finding it is read from says why that is not ten experiments.

## 3. What it cannot settle

- **Ten rolls of one world**: the card's own, so other worlds and the corpus's other suites -- where `e276` took its
  **3.30** to **11.84** range -- are not in the clause.
- **And one pair per roll**: each roll pairs the buffer against **one** anchor, so the clause compares the two anchors
  through the buffer and not directly, and the two anchors' own separation is not in it.
- **And a clause is not a result**: DA1 shows revision 9 survives into revision 10 and not that revision 9 was right, and
  DA2 shows the clause agrees with the ten rolls and not that the ten should be believed.
- **And the basis question is untouched**: the corpus was built to ask whether EWC should anchor in the neuron coordinate
  basis, and this clause states that the contrast which resolves is the one between **storing** and **anchoring**, not the
  one between two anchoring bases -- so the card now carries its own best answer to a question it does not ask.
