## 2. What the clause says

**The card now names the boundary its last three clauses were read across, and the boundary cuts the two anchors
apart.** On the earned label both anchors' newest-task costs resolve and agree -- **-0.1177** at **3.71** and **-0.1135**
at **4.49**, **0.0042** apart -- while on the neurons **only the matched-random one does** (**-0.0323** at **2.77**
against **-0.0115** at **0.78**) and the two are **0.0208** apart. So the clause's own content is that **the read-out is
not a scoping the card can apply to *the anchor*: it separates the pair**, and a reader of the `ledger` clause alone, who
would take the price for the arm's property, would be taking it for one read-out's and one arm's at once.

**And the anchors' standings and the basis contrast are indifferent to the boundary, which is what makes it a boundary
rather than a confound.** The two standings are **0.0063** apart on the earned label and **0.0083** on the neurons --
both inside the **0.0042** to **0.0108** range the pair units have measured on the earned label alone -- and the basis
contrast is a null on the diagonal at **0.36** and **0.49** sigma and on forgetting at **1.24** and **0.32**. So **four
of the six numbers the clause carries move by less than the pair's own separation when the head is changed**, and only
the two prices move by more: the read-out is a manipulation of the price and not of the pairing.

**And the two widths the clause records are `e286`'s axis inside it.** The earned label's read-out is **8** columns and the
neurons' is **32**, so the **0.0208** by which the prices separated is a difference between two heads that differ in what
they read **and** by a factor of four in width, and `e448` and `e449` measured the width axis on the earned label alone
(**4**, **8** and **16**). A reader who wants the price's dependence on the read-out separated from its dependence on the
width has to read this clause beside the `width` clause, and the two do not separate the two effects.

**And every number in the clause is recomputed rather than quoted, and the revision is additive as the last four were.**
DD2 takes the four runs' replicate lists and recomputes each standing, each price and both basis contrasts to a
thousandth of a point; DD1 checks that revision 10 survives into revision 11 in all **24** of its fields. So the card's
chain of clauses is now eleven revisions long with every one of them verified against the roll it was read from.

## 3. What it cannot settle

- **Two read-outs** of the four configurations the corpus runs, and the two differ in what they read **and** in width
  (**8** against **32**), so `e448`'s and `e449`'s width axis is inside this clause's difference rather than separated
  from it.
- **And one cell each**: the card's world at twenty replicates, so the other five draws and the three streams are not in
  the clause, and a price at **2.77** sigma against one at **0.78** is a pair two redraws could reorder.
- **And one partition draw**: the matched-random cells are one draw of the same group sizes, so *the matched-random arm's
  price is the neuron head's* is a statement about one draw of it.
- **And a clause is not a result**: DD1 shows revision 10 survives into revision 11 and not that revision 10 was right,
  and DD2 shows the clause agrees with the four rolls and not that the four should be believed.
