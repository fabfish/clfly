## 2. What moving the read-out says

**The anchor's one resolved effect on the card's world is the earned label's.** On the earned label it pays the newest
task **-0.1177** at **3.71** sigma; on the circuit's own neurons it pays **-0.0115** at **0.78**, and its whole-diagonal
standing reads **+0.0187** at **1.03** -- so **with the neuron read-out the anchor has no resolved effect in any of the
three currencies this line reads it in**, and the card's clauses about that arm are clauses about the earned-label
configuration and not about anchoring. That is a smaller and much more specific claim than revision 10 carries, and it is
the first time this line has found a reading of its own world that does not travel to the corpus's other read-out.

**And the reading it replaces is the sensible one.** The earned label puts the answer in the head's input: `naive` reads
the world's own state and fits the newest task directly, so an arm that regularises the **body** loses the newest task by
more -- and `e440` is the reason, since the anchor holds the recurrent weights harder than the corpus's typical penalty
cell while this world's task is read off sixteen fewer columns than the neuron read-out's thirty-two. Removing the earned
label removes the head's advantage and with it the price.

**And the buffer is ahead under both heads, by less.** `replay` over the anchor is **+0.1896** at **10.89** sigma on the
earned label and **+0.1090** at **7.66** on the neurons -- still four times the two-sigma bar, and the **0.0806** by
which the two read-outs differ is larger than the whole span of the **ten rolls** `e453` read on the earned label
(**0.0708**). So the method contrast is the robust headline on **both** read-outs and the read-out is a larger
manipulation of it than any of the five the ten rolls carry -- which qualifies revision 10's clause without contradicting
it: the contrast holds everywhere, and how large it is is the head's.

**And the buffer's own ledger holds under both, and the neuron read-out forgets less.** The three positions read
**+0.2323**, **+0.1812** and **-0.0302** against the earned label's **+0.2896**, **+0.2490** and **-0.0604**, so the
shape survives the read-out and every one of the three is smaller; and all three arms forget less on the neurons
(**0.2760**, **0.2354**, **0.0635** against **0.3750**, **0.3276**, **0.0906**), with the buffer's own forgetting
**0.0635** the smallest number this line has recorded for any arm on this world. The card's clauses about the buffer
therefore travel to the corpus's other read-out in shape and not in size, which is what `e450`'s width clause says of
another manipulation.

## 3. What it cannot settle

- **One read-out moved, two things changed**: the circuit's **32** neurons against the world's **8** differ in what they
  read **and** in width, and `e448`/`e449` measured the width axis on the earned label alone -- so the **0.0806** by
  which the method contrast fell is not separated here from the **8**-to-**32** widening it contains.
- **And one cell**: the card's world at twenty replicates, so the other five draws and the three streams are not in the
  reading, and an anchor's price at **0.78** sigma against **3.71** is a difference two redraws could shrink.
- **And one anchor**: `ewc-block-rand` is absent, though `e439` found the two **0.0042** apart at the last position and
  `e440` found them holding the weights **0.0046** apart -- and if the price is the earned label's rather than the
  anchoring's, the matched-random arm's price should be the earned label's too, which is a reading this unit does not
  take.
- **And a read-out is not a mechanism**: that the anchor's standing is unresolved under **both** heads says the body's
  regularisation does not move the diagonal either way, and not that it does nothing -- `e440`'s drift and bias ratios
  are the corpus's own account of what it does.
